    About

    The lab uses two models. Grain-scale scenarios (castings, single-crystal blade) use a cellular automaton on an 80 × 50 mm specimen. Dendrite-scale scenarios use a phase-field model on a window a few tens of micrometres wide.

    Grain scale: cellular automaton

    Nucleation. Each melt cell may hold a potential nucleus with a critical undercooling drawn from a Gaussian distribution (mean ΔTn, spread σ). Separate populations: untreated melt, grain-refined melt, mould walls, chills.

    Growth. Rappaz–Gandin cellular automaton (0.25 mm cells). Each grain grows as a square envelope whose corners are the <100> dendrite tips, at v = A·ΔT² with A = D/(π²·Γ·ΔT₀). The decentred-square rule keeps misoriented grains behind well-aligned ones.

    Heat flow. Casting mode solves heat conduction on a 1 mm grid with mould materials, interface heat-transfer coefficients and latent heat (Scheil release, eutectic plateau); filling is instant and there is no melt flow. Directional mode imposes T = TL + G·(s − V·t) with V = Ṫ/G.

    Dendrite scale: phase field

    Quantitative dilute-binary model with anti-trapping current (Karma 2001; Echebarria, Folch, Karma & Plapp 2004). It solves the phase field φ and the solute supersaturation U, with 4-fold interface anisotropy ε₄ and small fluctuations that trigger side-branching. Equiaxed: uniform temperature cooling at Ṫ. Columnar: fixed window with a frozen temperature field of gradient G moving at V, starting from seed crystals on a chilled wall; the run ends when the window has filled with dendrites. A grain label carries each crystal’s orientation. It runs on the GPU (WebGL2) with a smaller CPU fallback. The coupling constant λ = 25 keeps the dendrite tip radius at about 8 interface widths (roughly 10 cells), which the model needs to hold the crystal anisotropy; an arm is then about 20 cells wide, so the default windows are 1536 × 1536 cells (equiaxed, about 20 dendrites) and 1024 × 1280 cells (columnar). Results are qualitative to semi-quantitative.

    Limitations

    Not included: melt flow and grain movement, the eutectic reaction at the end of freezing (remaining liquid stays as enriched liquid), 3D effects, shrinkage porosity. Phase diagrams are linearised; the Ni superalloy is a pseudo-binary approximation.

    Credits

    Written for this course. The methods follow published work: Rappaz & Gandin (1993) for the cellular automaton, with reference to µMatIC from Imperial College London; Karma (2001) and Echebarria, Folch, Karma & Plapp (2004) for the phase-field model; Kurz & Fisher for the primary-spacing estimate.
